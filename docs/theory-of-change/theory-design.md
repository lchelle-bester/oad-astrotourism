## The diagram, transcribed

Please check this against the picture (or ask Joy to): it is the source for everything on the page. The words are OAD's. The diagram sets "Key assumptions" in a column at the right, level with the stage each belongs to. They are shown here under their stage.

**Title:** ASTROTOURISM THEORY OF CHANGE

**Problem statement** (the figure is highlighted in yellow in the diagram)
In 2023, the global unemployment rate was 5.1%.
Assumption: SDG 8.9: By 2030, devise and implement policies to promote sustainable tourism that creates jobs and promotes local culture and products. Indicators: 8.9.1: Tourism direct GDP as a proportion of total GDP and in growth rate.

**Key audience** (3)
1. Unemployed people in rural, semi-urban, urban areas with astrotourism potential
2. Under-employed tourism workers
3. Existing tourism businesses and tourism companies

Assumptions: Existing tourism businesses want to supplement their offerings. Unemployed target audience wants to start businesses.

**Stakeholders** (6)
1. Observatories, ROADs, IAU offices, broader astronomy and dark sky conservation community
2. Tourism ministries / government institutions
3. NGOs working with rural communities, capacity building programmes
4. Funders / Investors
5. Trainers / program facilitators
6. Tourism aggregators, promoters like Booking.com, (the comma is in the diagram)

Assumptions: Observatories are interested in community development initiatives. Astrotourism policies/strategies. Astronomy/dark sky community will champion astrotourism.

**Activities** (4)
1. Provide training to trainers who can then disseminate knowledge to communities and/or recorded training e.g. testing online resources
2. Develop and share resources (funding, support/workshops, etc.) to enable astrotourism businesses
3. Establish and nurture a global astrotourism network (including observatories) and a trained astrotourism ambassadors network
4. Reports

Assumptions: Trainers will be able to conduct high quality, effective trainings to the target audience using our resources and support. Funders and investors will be interested in funding such initiatives. An astrotourism network will help facilitate the exchange of best practices within the industry.

**Outputs** (4)
1. Number of trainers trained
2. Number of resources developed and used, number of training workshops held globally
3. Number of active members from various communities (e.g. funders, ambassadors, observatories, astrotourism community, etc.)
4. Research

Assumptions: More trained trainers will lead to wider reach and dissemination of astrotourism skills. Lack of funding is a barrier to starting an astrotourism businesses (sic). Having a database of funders/investors will help match people to relevant opportunities.

**Outcomes** (3)
1. Widespread awareness and a larger capacity to upskill people in astrotourism globally
2. New astrotourism businesses are developed in rural, semi-urban and urban areas
3. New opportunities available for astrotourism businesses as a result of collaboration with financial and tourism sectors. Heightened awareness of astrotourism as a viable business opportunity.

Assumptions: The resources and trainings will help build astrotourism and business skills. Interdisciplinary collaboration will increase effectiveness and reach of astrotourism. New businesses will lead to economic growth and development.

**Goals** (the three SDG boxes)
- PRIMARY SDG 8.9 (DECENT WORK AND ECONOMIC GROWTH): By 2030, devise and implement policies to promote sustainable tourism that creates jobs and promotes local culture and products. Indicators: 8.9.1 Tourism direct GDP as a proportion of total GDP and in growth rate
- SECONDARY SDG 10.2 (Reduced Inequalities): By 2030, empower and promote the social, economic and political inclusion of all, irrespective of age, sex, disability, race, ethnicity, origin, religion or economic or other status. Indicators 10.2.1 Proportion of people living below 50 per cent of median income, by sex, age and persons (the diagram's box cuts the text off here; the UN indicator ends "with disabilities")
- SECONDARY SDG 15 (LIFE ON LAND): Aims to protect terrestrial ecosystems, astrotourism promotes reducing light pollution to access dark skies. As a result, we will safeguard nocturnal wildlife and natural habitats. By fostering responsible stargazing and minimizing artificial light, it helps preserve local ecosystems and biodiversity, contributing to environmental conservation.

**Impact/Super impact**
Increased entrepreneurship and employment opportunities, along with a growing awareness of sustainable practices, are driving positive change, including improvements in light management, reducing light pollution and promoting more efficient use of resources.

**Definition**
The World Tourism Organization defines sustainable tourism as "tourism that takes full account of its current and future economic, social and environmental impacts, addressing the needs of visitors, the industry, the environment and host communities".

## What the diagram's structure really is

It is a chain read from top to bottom: problem, audience, stakeholders, activities, outputs, outcomes, goals, impact. Below the stakeholders the chain splits into **parallel pathways**, which the arrows show:
- **Pathway 1:** training of trainers, then the number of trainers trained, then widespread awareness and a larger capacity to upskill people.
- **Pathway 2:** developing and sharing resources, then the number of resources and workshops, then new astrotourism businesses.
- **Pathway 3:** establishing and nurturing a network, then the number of active members, then new opportunities through collaboration with the financial and tourism sectors.
- **Pathway 4:** Reports, then Research. It has no outcome in the diagram.

The assumptions are per stage, not per pathway. The three goals sit under all the outcomes, and the impact under the goals.
